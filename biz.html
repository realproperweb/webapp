The 5 main reasons South African companies are failing

Drawn from the Absa/SACCI/BMR Small Business Growth Index, University of the Western Cape research, Stats SA liquidations and multiple 2025 reports (2024–2026).

1

Cash-flow mismanagement and chronic liquidity crises

The single biggest killer. Businesses look profitable on paper but run out of cash. Rising costs, slow-paying customers and no real-time visibility is a deadly mix.

60% report weak or critical cash flow
2

Exploding cost of doing business, with no efficiency offset

Loadshedding, electricity tariffs, fuel and transport, input inflation and infrastructure failures. Most owners absorb the pain or raise prices, which kills demand. Only top performers redesign costs and productivity.

66%+ of SMEs hit hardest by fuel and transport
3

Weak customer acquisition in a shrinking market

Lower consumer spending and more competition. Many SMEs rely on word-of-mouth or random social posts, with no consistent, measurable lead engine. When the economy tightens, the pipeline dries up.

No lead engine = no pipeline
4

Manual systems, zero automation and technology lag

A top endogenous failure factor. Spreadsheets, WhatsApp chaos, paper invoices and the founder as the system. It causes errors, slows everything down, burns out the owner and makes the business impossible to scale or sell.

Painful work also hurts talent attraction
5

Founder dependence and no scalable processes

The business dies when the founder steps away or burns out. No systems, no delegation, no succession readiness. Add skills shortages and regulatory drag, and the company can’t survive shocks.

3 in 5 failures happen despite reported profits
52.8%

The result: more than half of small businesses are currently contracting, trading with difficulty or at risk of closure. Over half may not survive another 12 months under current pressures.
